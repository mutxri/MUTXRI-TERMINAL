USER GUIDE

Drive the terminal from the first keystroke.

MX Terminal is keyboard first. You type a ticker, a panel code, or both, and the workspace follows. This page is the whole vocabulary.

The layout

Four windows, one strip of workspaces, and a command line. Everything you type lands in one of them.

TOP BARBrand, the command line, the market in front, the clock
TAPEScrolling prices for the exchange you are on
WATCHLISTEvery security on this exchange. Code, name, price, change. Click a row to load it.
STAGEWhere a panel opens. Panels float over the workspace in their own windows, and several stay open at once.
MARKETThe quote sheet for whatever security you selected, and where a command that does not resolve answers in words.
CHATThe room window in the right column, for signed-in readers
WORKSPACESThe sheet strip along the foot of the window. MAIN on a first visit; the + adds another.

The command line is the search box at the top of the screen, the wide one reading "Ticker, company or market". Click it, type, press Enter. Your text is not case sensitive.

What to type

Four patterns cover the everyday work.

PatternExampleWhat happens
HELP HELP Lists every panel code in the rail on the right.
A ticker SOL Opens that company's quote sheet in the MARKET window: price, change, volume, sector, country, chief executive, competitors, customers, partners, suppliers, shares in issue, market cap and major shareholders.
A panel code HEATMAP Opens that panel in its own window, on the exchange you are currently viewing.
Ticker then panel ABG FINANCIALS Opens the panel already scoped to that company. This is the fastest route to a single company's statements.

A company name works wherever a ticker does. Anything the terminal cannot resolve comes back as a short error in the rail rather than a blank screen.

Switching market

You never have to leave the command line to change exchange.

PatternExampleWhat happens
An exchangeNGXSwitches the whole board to that market. JSE, NGX, EGX.
A country codeKESame thing by country: ZA, NG, KE, EG.
Ticker then marketMTN JSESwitches market and opens that company, in one go.
Market then panelEGX NEWSSwitches market and opens the panel there, at market level.

One exception worth knowing. NSE and JSE are each both an exchange and a company listed on it, so typing one on its own opens the stock, not the market. Use the country code instead: KE for Nairobi, ZA for Johannesburg.

Workspaces

The strip along the foot of the window holds your workspaces. A first visit has one, MAIN, and it holds WATCHLIST, MARKET, FOCUS and CHAT.

The other six sets are not tabs. They are behind the + at the right end of the strip: START HERE, INVESTOR, TRADER, ANALYST, FUND MANAGER and ECONOMIST. Picking one appends it, so it never replaces the workspace you already have. Anything you add from the + is yours: it is saved, and it comes back on the next visit. A guest's arrangement lasts while the page stays open, and the strip says so.

ControlWhat happens
MAINThe house arrangement: WATCHLIST down the left, MARKET the largest in the middle, FOCUS top right, CHAT under it. It re-tiles to the window you actually have until you move something.
+Adds a workspace. The menu offers an empty one, or one of the six sets above.
A tabSwitches to that workspace. Each one keeps its own window places, sizes and stacking order.
Double click a tabRenames it.
x on a tabDeletes it. One workspace always stays.
Dragging a windowThat workspace stops being automatic and keeps the arrangement you gave it.

Paper trading

The TRADE panel has two tabs. PAPER is a practice account: orders are simulated against the published prices, no fees or taxes are modelled, and nothing is sent to a broker, because MX Terminal is not one. The account is held in your own browser, one per exchange, in that market's currency. LIVE says what a real brokerage account would need on that market; live orders are not available.

The ticket takes a side, a quantity, an order type and, where the type needs them, a price and a time in force. Market orders fill at the latest published price. Limit, stop and stop-limit orders rest under working orders until a session reaches them. DAY orders expire after one session and GTC orders carry over. RESET PAPER ACCOUNT clears the book for that exchange.

Why a panel can be empty. These markets are end of day, so where no intraday print exists TAS says so instead of drawing a tape. BND, DER and GLCO read published snapshots rather than a live feed, because no free real-time feed exists for them, and a value the source does not publish is left blank with its reason rather than filled in.

HOLDERS and RESEARCH are no longer panels. The holder rows sit in the MARKET window and in FOCUS, and the research stack opens in the MARKET window when you click a security there.

The chart panel

CHART opens a chart for the security in focus, with its own search field in the header. Type a ticker, or part of a company name, and a list of matching securities appears under the field as you type. It is not case sensitive.

A ticker that starts with what you typed is listed first, then any other ticker or name that contains it. Only securities the selected exchange lists are offered, because the list is read from the same market file the chart draws from. Pick a row with the mouse, or move with the arrow keys and press Enter; Enter with no row highlighted opens the first match. Escape clears the field.

A query that matches nothing prints NO NSE SECURITY MATCHES "...", naming the exchange and what you typed, and the chart stays on the security it was showing. If you press Enter on words that match nothing, they are passed through as a ticker and the chart says it has no quote or no history for it rather than drawing something else.

On the landing page

The landing page now carries a display-only terminal in the hero, and one static panel view beside each of its eight feature sections. They are pictures of the product. Every frame is inert: nothing inside a display can be clicked, typed into or scrolled, and the search field drawn in the CHART window of the hero is part of the picture too. Open the terminal for the real thing.

Below 821px the page stacks. The desk inside the hero frame becomes one column of seven windows, in this order: MARKET, CHART, FOCUS, COMPARE, HEATMAP, MACRO, WATCHLIST. The frame takes the height of that column, so you scroll the page and the desk moves past with it.

The eight feature sections show the quote monitor, the chart, financials, the screener, the investor sheet, corporate actions, news and paper trading, each as one panel view on one exchange.

What the figures promise

Every figure the terminal prints carries its source and its unit, and the period it belongs to where the period matters. A figure the source does not publish prints as absent, in words or as a dash with the reason on it, and never as a zero. A period the source marks as a projection is labelled as a projection. A ratio that is withheld prints as withheld and says why. A panel with nothing behind it says so rather than filling the space.

Getting in

Open the terminal and it loads on the market you last used. Reading is open to anyone. Two things ask for an account: the paper book (TRADE and PORTFOLIO), and saving a layout, including the workspaces you add. From the landing page choose Login or Sign Up, then continue with Google or use an email and password. Google is the quickest route: pick the account and you land straight in the terminal with no new password to remember.
