Data Methodology

How the data in MUTXRI TERMINAL is sourced, normalised and checked, and the limits you should know before relying on it.

What this page is for

Financial data is only useful if you can tell where it came from and how far to trust it. This page states the provenance of every dataset, the checks that run against it, what is withheld when a check fails, and the markets where our coverage is currently weak. It is written from the measured state of the database, not from intent.

How gaps are labelled

A missing figure is never estimated, interpolated or carried forward from another period. Where a value is not available it is left empty and renders as a dash. Two consequences follow, and both are deliberate:

  • Coverage looks lower than it would if we guessed. Income statements are present for 847 of 938 securities rather than for all of them.
  • A statement can have a fuller row set than another for the same sector. That reflects what the issuer published, not a difference in effort.

Every figure carries its source. Each statement file records the source it came from, the currency, and the as-of year. Where a value was computed from other filed values rather than filed directly, it is derived by arithmetic on those filed values and never replaced by an estimate.

Automated checks, and what happens when one fails

  • Net profit above revenue. If profit exceeds revenue by more than 50 per cent the pair is impossible and both figures are withheld for that period, because the stored source does not allow deciding which of the two is wrong. 58 period-pairs across 44 statements are currently withheld on this ground, each logged with symbol, period, both values and source.
  • Profit above revenue within 50 per cent is retained. There are 34 such pairs. This is arithmetically possible for banks, where reported revenue is often net interest income, and for issuers with large one-off or associate income. They are flagged rather than blanked.
  • Balance sheet identity. Total assets must equal total liabilities plus total equity, within 0.5 per cent. 9 of 361 tested balance sheets currently fail this and are queued for re-collection from the source filing.
  • Ratio plausibility ceiling. Ratios such as return on equity, margin and debt to equity are recomputed from their inputs, and a ratio outside a plausible range indicates a bad input rather than a real result, so it is withheld rather than published.
  • Derived rows are only produced from identities verified against the stored data. Six accounting identities are used because they hold across the dataset; seven others were tested and rejected because they contradict the filed figures.

Executive records: the standard applied

A person is listed only where a source names both that security and the office held. The office is recorded exactly as published, and never promoted to a different title. A combined office, where one person holds both the chair and the executive role, is recorded as what it is rather than treated as a chief executive.

Where no executive office is published, the row is empty on purpose. Of the 938 listed securities, 778 resolve to a sourced executive record. Of the remainder, some publish no executive office at all, and the rest are notes, funds or preference shares, which have no chief executive by definition.

Known limitations

South African and Egyptian statements are the weakest link. 99.6 per cent of JSE and 75.6 per cent of EGX statement files currently come from a licensed secondary feed rather than from the issuer's own filing. That feed is labelled on every row, but it is not the same standard as the Kenyan and Nigerian data, which is extracted from the exchanges' filing libraries. Moving JSE and EGX to filing-level extraction is the current priority.

Price history is complete across all four boards. Every listed security now carries a stored daily series; Nigeria and Kenya were previously the thin spots and are now backfilled.

  • Not real time. Prices are end of day. There is no intraday tick data.
  • No order execution. The terminal does not place real orders and does not connect to a broker. Simulated orders exist for practice only.
  • Identical figures appear on more than one ticker in a small number of cases, which indicates an entity resolution problem rather than correct duplicate data. These are identified and queued.
  • Nine balance sheets fail the accounting identity and are listed above, pending re-collection.
  • Coverage figures on this page are measured from the database, and this page is regenerated from those measurements rather than edited by hand.

Reporting a problem

If you find a figure that looks wrong, send the security, the period and the figure. A wrong number is treated as a defect against this dataset and the row is withheld until it is sourced again. contact@mutxriterminal.com
